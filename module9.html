Data Visualization & Storytelling • Hands-on module

Google Looker Studio

From raw data to business metrics, interactive dashboards, and actionable stories

Suggested duration: 1–2 weeks   |   Level: Beginner to intermediate

1. Module overview and learning outcomes

Google Looker Studio is a browser-based reporting and dashboard tool. Google’s documentation may now refer to the product as Data Studio (formerly Looker Studio); the concepts in this module remain applicable. This is different from the enterprise product named Looker.

By the end of this module, students will be able to:

  • Explain the difference between dimensions, metrics, calculated fields, and KPIs.
  • Prepare and connect a Google Sheets data source.
  • Build and validate calculated metrics.
  • Design scorecards, time series, bar charts, tables, and interactive controls.
  • Interpret dashboard results and communicate recommendations.
  • Share a report safely and document design decisions.
Access: Use a Google account and visit lookerstudio.google.com. Interface wording may differ by account or product update.

2. Concepts: data, dimensions, metrics, and KPIs

TermMeaningSales example
DimensionCategory or attribute used to group resultsProduct, Region, Order Date
MetricNumerical measure that can be aggregatedRevenue, Units Sold
Calculated fieldNew field defined with a formulaRevenue = Quantity × Unit Price
KPIMetric selected to measure progress toward a goalRevenue vs. monthly target
DashboardOrganized set of visualizations supporting decisionsSales performance overview

Example business question: Which product categories generate the most revenue, and is sales performance improving over time?

A strong KPI includes a definition, calculation, timeframe, target, and business interpretation. A chart is not automatically a KPI.

3. Getting started: create and connect a report

  1. Open Looker Studio and sign in.
  2. Prepare a Google Sheet with one header row, no merged cells, consistent data types, and one transaction per row.
  3. Choose Create → Report (or start a blank report).
  4. In the data connection dialog, select the Google Sheets connector.
  5. Choose your spreadsheet and worksheet; confirm the first row contains headers.
  6. Select Add or Connect, then confirm adding the data source to the report.
  7. Rename the report to Sales Performance Dashboard – Your Name.
  8. Check that the date field is recognized as a Date and numeric columns are numbers, not text.

5. Build your first calculated metrics

In the report, find your data source under Resource → Manage added data sources → Edit (menu names may vary). Choose Add a field → Add calculated field. Enter each name and formula, then save.

Field nameFormulaInterpretation
RevenueQuantity * Unit_PriceSales amount per transaction
CostQuantity * Unit_CostCost of goods per transaction
ProfitQuantity * (Unit_Price - Unit_Cost)Gross profit per transaction

To calculate an overall Profit Margin %, add a chart-level field with:

SUM(Profit) / SUM(Revenue)

Set its display type to Percent. Do not multiply by 100 if the field is formatted as a percentage. Aggregating the numerator and denominator first produces the correct weighted overall margin.

6. Build a dashboard: step by step

  1. Scorecards: Select Add a chart → Scorecard. Make separate cards for SUM(Revenue), SUM(Profit), SUM(Quantity), and Profit Margin %. Format currency and percent correctly.
  2. Time series: Add a time-series chart; use Order_Date as the date dimension and Revenue as the metric. Sort chronologically.
  3. Bar chart: Add a bar chart with Category as dimension and Revenue as metric. Sort descending by revenue.
  4. Regional comparison: Add a bar chart with Region as dimension and Profit as metric.
  5. Detail table: Add a table with Product, Revenue, Quantity, and Profit.
  6. Interactive controls: Add a dropdown filter for Region and a date-range control; verify that compatible charts respond.
  7. Polish: Add a clear title, consistent number formats, accessible contrast, labels, and a short insight summary.
  8. Test: Switch to View mode, change filters, verify totals, and check for missing values.

8. Hands-on assignment — Build a business dashboard

Scenario: You are a junior business analyst asked to create an interactive sales dashboard for a retail manager. The manager needs to understand revenue, profitability, product performance, and regional differences.

Requirements

  1. Create or extend the supplied Google Sheets dataset to at least 30 realistic transaction rows spanning at least two months, three categories, and three regions.
  2. Connect your data to Looker Studio and verify column types.
  3. Create and use the Revenue, Cost, Profit, and Profit Margin % calculations.
  4. Create at least four KPI scorecards: Revenue, Profit, Units Sold, and Profit Margin %.
  5. Include at least three distinct chart types, including a time series, a category comparison, and a product detail table.
  6. Add at least two interactive controls: Region and date range.
  7. Include a short dashboard narrative: three findings supported by numbers and two recommended business actions.
  8. Use clear labels, consistent currency/percent formatting, and a logical visual hierarchy.
  9. Test the dashboard in View mode and confirm at least one metric manually from the source data.

Submission

  • A view-only report link accessible to the instructor (do not make it public if not necessary).
  • A PDF export or screenshots of the completed dashboard.
  • A link to the Google Sheet or an exported CSV dataset.
  • A 1–2 page written reflection explaining metric formulas, design decisions, three findings, and two recommendations.
  • At least four screenshots documenting: data connection, a calculated field, dashboard in edit mode, and final dashboard in view mode.

10. Troubleshooting and best practices

  • Dates not grouping correctly? Check the source sheet's date formatting and the field type in the data source.
  • Metric shows Count instead of Sum? Check the field type and aggregation settings.
  • Profit margin is wrong? Use SUM(Profit)/SUM(Revenue), not an unweighted average of row margins.
  • Charts do not respond to filters? Verify they use compatible data sources and that control scope is correct.
  • Data is missing? Check date ranges, filters, blank cells, and data refresh.
  • Report link not opening? Confirm the instructor has viewer permission and that data credentials allow the report to render.

Use consistent colors, avoid unnecessary 3D effects, label axes, keep chart counts manageable, and write an insight headline rather than merely naming a chart.

13. Business intelligence metrics and KPI design

Dimension: a descriptive category such as Region. Metric: a quantitative measurement such as Revenue. KPI: a metric linked to a specific target, such as monthly revenue ≥ $50,000.

MetricExample formula / methodQuestion answered
Total revenueSUM(Revenue)How much did we sell?
Total profitSUM(Profit)How much profit was generated?
Profit marginSUM(Profit) / SUM(Revenue); format as PercentWhat share of revenue became profit?
Average order valueSUM(Revenue) / COUNT_DISTINCT(Order_ID)What is the average revenue per order?
Conversion rateSUM(Conversions) / SUM(Sessions); format as PercentWhat fraction of sessions converted?
Revenue per customerSUM(Revenue) / COUNT_DISTINCT(Customer_ID)How much revenue per unique customer?
Sales growth(Current_Period_Revenue - Previous_Period_Revenue) / Previous_Period_RevenueHow did revenue change? Use a period comparison or correctly aligned source fields.

Important: Looker Studio's Percent number format normally multiplies a fractional result by 100 for display. Do not multiply a formula by 100 and apply Percent formatting. Guard against zero denominators and missing values. Period growth needs comparable periods, not simply two unrelated columns.

Guided KPI exercise

  1. Identify a business question, the corresponding metric, and a measurable target.
  2. In the data source or chart, select Add a field and name the new metric Profit Margin.
  3. Enter SUM(Profit) / SUM(Revenue), check validity, save, and set the field type to Percent.
  4. Add a scorecard for Profit Margin and explain whether the result meets your chosen goal.
  5. Repeat for Average Order Value and Conversion Rate if the required source columns exist.

14. Dashboard design and data storytelling

A dashboard is a decision-making tool, not a collection of unrelated charts. Begin with the audience's decision and build a visual narrative around it.

  1. Lead with the answer: put the most important KPIs at the top, alongside targets or comparisons.
  2. Show change: use time-series charts for trends and bars for category comparisons.
  3. Explain drivers: break down results by region, product, channel or customer segment.
  4. Guide action: add a concise insight caption and recommendation supported by the data.
  5. Design accessibly: use readable text, clear labels, sufficient contrast and cues beyond color alone.

15. Common errors and troubleshooting challenges

  1. Using Sum instead of Average, or Count instead of Sum.
  2. Combining text, date and numeric values without compatible types.
  3. Multiplying a rate by 100 and then formatting it as Percent.
  4. Joining tables with duplicate keys and inflating totals.
  5. Using pie charts for too many categories or line charts for unordered categories.
  6. Adding too many charts, colors or controls to one page.
  7. Assuming report sharing automatically grants access to underlying data.
  8. Ignoring null values, inconsistent date ranges or missing records.

Debugging task: A dashboard reports $240,000 revenue, but the original sales sheet totals $120,000. Inspect the blend join keys, row duplication and chart aggregation. Document the root cause and fix with before-and-after screenshots.
